# Expressibility

Expressibility is quantified by the Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence of the fidelity distribution of randomly sampled states (evaluated at $\boldsymbol{x} = \boldsymbol{0}$) from the Haar reference distribution, where lower values indicate higher expressibility.
For mixed states, the squared Uhlmann fidelity is used while the pure-state Haar reference is retained, so changes in the divergence can reflect changes in state exploration as well as mixedness.
The least expressive case, i.e. the empty circuit, yields the upper bound $(2^n - 1)\ln(n_\text{bins})$ with $n_\text{bins} = 75 n$ equal-width fidelity bins for $n$ qubits.
The abbreviations of the ansätze and noise types are explained on the [Setup](setup.md) page.

## Expressibility under Noise

Our Figure 7 in the paper:
![Expressibility](figures/expr_light.png#only-light)
![Expressibility](figures/expr_dark.png#only-dark)

KL divergence of the fidelity distribution from the Haar reference under increasing noise levels, with lower values indicating higher expressibility. Points represent the mean over the five seeds. The dotted lines mark the upper bound $(2^n-1)\ln(75n)$ for each qubit count and hence the least expressive case.

Only the mean over the five seeds is shown, as the relative standard deviation across seeds is below 5% on average and reaches at most 29%, the latter only for coherent gate errors.

- **Without noise, the ranking follows the ansatz structure.** The SEA is the most expressive ansatz for four or more qubits, followed by the HEA and CRA, while the RYA shows the largest KL divergence, as it generates only states with real amplitudes at zero input.
- **Decoherent gate errors equalise the expressibility.** BF, PF and DP increase the KL divergence by up to three orders of magnitude already at 0.5%, and at 3% all ansätze lie within about one order of magnitude of the respective upper bound. The noiseless ranking of the SEA and the CRA is reversed, consistent with the number of noisy gates per layer, but noise level and qubit count dominate the KL divergence.
- **SPAM and damping errors have a small effect.** At 3%, they increase the KL divergence to values of order one for SPAM errors and of order 0.1 to 0.5 for damping errors, one to two orders of magnitude below those for decoherent gate errors. For the RYA, small levels of SP, ME and PD even reduce the KL divergence slightly, as the measure does not distinguish between a Haar-like distribution generated by the ansatz and one caused by mixing.
- **Coherent gate errors have little effect on the sampled expressibility.** The CGE curves remain close to their noiseless values over the tested levels.

## Expressibility of Deeper Circuits

Our Figure 16 in the paper (Appendix "Expressibility of Deeper Circuits"):
![Expressibility for deeper circuits](figures/expr_layers_light.png#only-light)
![Expressibility for deeper circuits](figures/expr_layers_dark.png#only-dark)

KL divergence of the fidelity distribution from the Haar reference for $L \in \{1, 2, 4\}$ encoding layers at a fixed noise level of 3% for each noise type; lower values indicate higher expressibility. The first column shows the noiseless reference. Points represent the mean over the five seeds. The dotted lines mark the upper bound for the KL divergence at a certain qubit count and hence the least expressive case. The values for $L = 1$ correspond to the 3% points in Fig. 7.

Without noise and under coherent gate errors, the KL divergence of the SEA does not change with depth, whereas the HEA and the CRA decrease by about one order of magnitude from one to two layers for four and more qubits and then level off at the values of the SEA.
The RYA remains at its single-layer value of about 0.2 for all depths, as additional real-valued gates do not lift the restriction to real amplitudes.
Under decoherent gate errors, the KL divergence increases monotonically with depth for all ansätze and qubit counts and approaches the upper bound, while SPAM and damping errors act only once per qubit at the circuit boundary and are therefore independent of depth.
